0.8.0 · Engine

One change, everywhere it should apply.

0.8.0 is the release where "engine, not just an extension" stopped being a slogan and started being true in the code. A field change across many notes now works identically from the CLI, the API, VS Code, and Conduit — one shared module, not four reimplementations. The query language learned to walk the graph and look into the past. Automation survives closing the terminal. And the vault started noticing its own shape and its own habits, not just yours.

Twenty-two real, shipped changes this cycle, every one of them below — no filler, and nothing held back for its own separate showcase.

What shipped

All twenty-two, not a curated highlight reel.

query & automation
VQL v1
Hops deep, cycle-safe: linked_to/linked_from ... within N real graph traversal inside !view. as of <date> reconstructs a query's results as they stood on a past date, on the same Time Engine already powering cat --at/graph --at.

Surfaces, one module: yamlink bulk-set, PATCH /api/nodes/bulk's { add } mode, VS Code's Bulk Set Field on Selected Notes, and Conduit's "Add to list field" all call the same src/core/bulkFieldOperation.js — one implementation, four doors into it.

The bug that spawned 1,400 processes
A real smoke test against a real vault produced over 1,400 live processes in under a minute — a worker silently re-entering supervisor mode and spawning its own worker doing the same, recursively. No unit test caught it, only running the real thing did. Fixed with one line, locked in with a regression test.

Three actions above every heading, task, quote, footnote: "Copy reference," "N references" (jumps straight to a backlink), and "Extract to note" (moves a block into its own note in one step).

yamlink on --daemon survives closing the terminal — a real supervisor respawns a crashed worker with backoff, and the watcher retries a flaky fs.watch failure instead of dying silently.

How this cycle actually went

I'm going to sound dramatic here, but honestly, it felt dramatic, lol.

This one took a long time to get through. And that's on me.

I had a plan — all centered around Conduit. I wanted to build a "timeline scrubber." This, in essence, would let you go through your vault's history and reconstruct any point in time, right from your terminal. I thought it was a really cool idea (still do). But as I started testing it and seeing it live, I just couldn't find a way to make it properly work. My main mistake was spending so much time on this one feature without ever really understanding whether I could achieve it or not. So that first element got killed off.

Then I had the idea to create a "canvas" inside the terminal — a "map" of your notes, with visual representation and navigation of all your work. But taking that vision into the functional reality of a terminal proved too difficult, especially working on a tight schedule. So, both ideas were busts — at least for now.

That's how I saw my 0.8.0 release completely butchered, lol.

Outside of Conduit work, a lot of the planned work was around QOL elements and codebase health — which, of course, isn't enough for a full release. So I decided to look into the roadmap and see what could be moved up and rearranged to make 0.8.0 a solid release. A query language that accomplishes "more," automation that survives closing the terminal — these things were not planned AT ALL for 0.8.0. It took quite some time and planning, it took a lot of tinkering, but I believe that Yamlink, no matter what, is stronger today than it was yesterday.

I insist, Conduit is a very important part of my plans, but my ideas just didn't hit. I will obviously look to improve and polish the experience moving forward. But for 0.8.0, it just wasn't meant to be.

And to be honest, what matters most is input. Input is the best way to grow and improve. If you could provide feedback, in any shape or form, I'd greatly appreciate it! Just go to our GH and you can leave your thoughts and opinions on any of the active threads.

Please, enjoy 0.8.0!

— J
August 2026
Insights

yamlink.dev is up! It took a while to come up with a concept I thought was worthy of Yamlink. Even though the entire project isn't done, there's now at least a proper version for everyone to get to know and use. There's still a few twists and turns I want to implement, but for now, I think the experience is pretty cool — I hope you like it!

I've also been taking a stab at writing. Right now there are 3 pieces up on dev.to, all related to use cases for your vault using Markdown and Yamlink. I'm not ready to call them "masterpieces," but I had fun writing them — maybe you'll enjoy them too:

  • Your MD as a fitness tracker
  • Can I have my entire CRM setup in Markdown?
  • Turning Markdown Files into a Queryable Knowledge Graph

I'm also toying with the idea of a Discord server. Right now there's not really a need for one — only a few people have commented on GitHub — but I feel like a space for real-time chat could make interaction and community-building easier down the line. I'm not 100% sold on it. If you have an opinion either way, let me know.

Cheers,
—j
